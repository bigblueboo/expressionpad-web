# ExpressionPad and the iOS expressive-control market

## Competitive analysis, product strategy, and launch brief

**Research snapshot:** July 25, 2026  
**Market:** US App Store, with global developer documentation and historical context  
**Scope:** iPhone/iPad expressive instruments, MPE/MIDI controllers, alternative keyboards and grids, modular controller builders, AUv3 hosts, and touch-native synths that compete for the same job  
**Status labels:** **Fact** means supported by repository or cited market evidence; **Interpretation** is the market read; **Recommendation** is proposed action.

---

## Executive summary

ExpressionPad has a real opening, but “customizable MIDI controller” is the wrong category to claim.

The blank-canvas controller market is already owned by TouchOSC, MIDI Designer Pro X, Lemur, and a new wave of inexpensive builders. The expressive keyboard market has credible incumbents in GeoShred, KB-1, Velocity Keyboard, ThumbJam, and Midi Poly Grid. New 2026 launches—including KeyPad, Hexatone, iotaTONE, and Rubberband—show that this is an active niche, not a museum of early iPad experiments.

ExpressionPad’s opportunity sits between those camps:

> **A continuous instrument that happens to speak MIDI.**

It combines configurable square, hexagonal, and stacked-piano geometries with per-finger pitch motion, Y-drag expression, fret snapping, spring vibrato, haptic semitone landmarks, a two-thumb phone mirror, internal synth and sampler voices, and MPE-style output. No current competitor found in this survey packages that exact interaction system. The repository’s feature set is unusually coherent when framed as an instrument; it is merely one more long feature list when framed as a controller. See the local [product README](../README.md) and [iOS architecture README](../ios/README.md).

The closest threats are different for different reasons:

- **GeoShred** is the performance-credibility and pitch-behavior benchmark.
- **KeyPad** is the newest broad “surface + sound + AUv3 + OSC” threat.
- **KB-1** is the polished multi-layout controller and AUv3 workflow benchmark.
- **Velocity Keyboard** is the glass-velocity benchmark.
- **Musix Pro** and **Hexatone** are the configurable-geometry and microtonal benchmarks.
- **Rubberband** is the most conceptually dangerous new entrant: a phone-first continuous pitch surface with attraction, tunings, tilt, haptics, sound, and MPE.
- **ThumbJam** is the enduring “play immediately and never hit a wrong note” benchmark.
- **Animoog Z**, **TC-11**, **MorphWiz Studio**, **Samplr**, **Borderlands**, and **Mononoke** are not direct controller substitutes, but they set expectations for what a touch-native instrument should feel and sound like.
- **TouchOSC** owns blank-canvas flexibility; **Loopy Pro**, **AUM**, and **LK** shape the host workflow into which ExpressionPad must fit.

The largest gap is **AUv3**. GeoShred, KB-1, Velocity Keyboard, KeyPad, Hexatone, Mononoke, Animoog Z, and other current products can participate in session-contained host workflows. ExpressionPad is currently a standalone iOS app. That makes AUv3 more than a checklist item: it is project recall, recordability, lower routing friction, and a signal that the product belongs in a modern iOS studio.

The second gap is **durable setup ownership**. The native app saves one state in `UserDefaults`; the web version can share URL configuration, but the product does not yet expose a named setup library, duplicate/save-as, import/export, iCloud sync, or versioned migration. In this category, presets are onboarding, performance recall, community content, and continuity insurance at once.

There is also a pre-launch truth problem to fix. The iOS README says “background-audio mode,” while the app explicitly stops audio and relinquishes its session on background because it has no background-playback feature. Marketing must not promise background performance until the implementation, entitlement, lifecycle behavior, and documentation agree. Likewise, the surface’s “pressure” is generated by vertical drag after onset, not independent physical Z-force. The honest, stronger description is “per-finger vertical expression” or “motion-derived aftertouch.”

### Recommended launch decision

Do not launch the native app as a premium professional controller until these are true:

1. Background behavior is intentionally specified, implemented, and tested.
2. MPE output is tested end to end against a published compatibility list, including bend-range setup, voice stealing, reconnect, and panic.
3. Users can save, duplicate, name, export, import, and restore complete instrument setups.
4. The first-run experience makes sound immediately and teaches tap, slide, vertical expression, frets, and mirror mode in under a minute.
5. AUv3 is either shipped or explicitly put on a near-term public roadmap. A launch without it can work as an “instrument preview” or lower-priced first release, but not as the complete pro workflow story.

If AUv3 and setup ownership ship at launch, **$14.99 one-time** is the clearest price. If they do not, use a free playable/controller tier with a **$14.99 Pro unlock**, or launch at **$7.99–$9.99** and raise the price with the host-ready release. Avoid a subscription unless a genuine continuing content or cloud service exists.

## 1. Method and confidence

This analysis screened more than 60 products across seven parallel research tracks. It profiled direct controllers, grid and microtonal surfaces, touch-native instruments, modular builders, hosts/workstations, App Store merchandising, and user/community discourse. Current price, availability, version, and update date were checked against live US Apple metadata on July 25, 2026 wherever possible. Capability claims prefer first-party manuals and product pages; specialist reviews and community reports are used for workflow and reputation, with anecdotal limits noted.

The 27-app merchandising sample is purposive, not a census of the App Store. Its statistics describe this competitive set, not all music apps. Current storefront metadata also cannot establish latency, connection reliability, MPE conformance, accessibility quality, or equal usability on every supported device. Those require hands-on testing.

Historical products are separated from current purchase choices. The original ExpressionPad, Pen2Bow, Aftertouch, AC Sabre, and ROLI NOISE are not treated as live US competitors when Apple returns no current product record.

## 2. What ExpressionPad is actually selling

### Product baseline — facts from the repository

ExpressionPad is both a web instrument/controller and a native Swift iOS app. Its core playable system includes:

- independent touch voices with onset velocity, continuous per-note pitch bend, and vertical-drag expression;
- square, hexagonal, and stacked-piano surfaces with arbitrary rows and columns, row tunings, column scales, root note, and portrait/landscape support;
- a slide continuum from discrete retriggering to continuous pitch, optional semitone frets, and spring-centered in-key vibrato;
- a mirrored phone layout that makes the same geometry converge toward both thumbs;
- haptic ticks at semitone crossings;
- optional tilt routing;
- an additive synth, generated sampler instruments, one user sample, and four effects;
- full-screen performance mode;
- MPE-style MIDI 1.0 output using a channel per touch, pitch bend, channel pressure, and CC74;
- simple MIDI input to the internal engine;
- web URL configuration and local persistence.

These are documented in the [product README](../README.md). The native implementation uses AVAudioEngine, CoreMIDI, and a custom Swift DSP kernel, as described in the [iOS README](../ios/README.md).

### The defensible unit is the interaction system

None of the individual ingredients is unique:

- grids are common;
- MPE is expected in the expressive niche;
- internal synths and samplers are common;
- haptics, tilt, scale locks, and microtonality all appear elsewhere;
- controller customization is highly commoditized.

The defensible unit is how the ingredients behave together:

1. choose a musically meaningful lattice;
2. decide whether it behaves as keys, frets, or a continuous field;
3. bend and vibrate every finger independently;
4. feel pitch crossings without watching;
5. play the same instrument with two thumbs on a phone;
6. hear it immediately or route the same gestures to an MPE destination.

That is a product story. A list of MIDI messages is not.

### Current exposure — facts from the code and documentation

| Area | Current state | Competitive consequence |
|---|---|---|
| AUv3 | Standalone app; no AUv3 extension documented | Largest workflow and credibility gap |
| Setup library | One persisted state; no named banks, save-as, or native import/export | Weak recall, sharing, backup, and community loop |
| Background operation | Documentation claims a mode, while lifecycle code stops audio in background | Must resolve before any claim |
| MIDI protocol | MIDI 1.0 channel-rotating MPE-style output | Sufficient for launch; MIDI 2.0 is not urgent |
| MIDI input | Simple note input to the engine | Not a full MPE sound-module workflow |
| Expression semantics | “Pressure” is vertical motion after onset | Market as Y expression/aftertouch, not physical pressure |
| Microtonality | Tunings/scales and bend exist, but no Scala/EDO authoring workflow | Behind a fast-growing 2026 subsegment |
| Open control | No OSC, Audiobus, Ableton Link, or AUv3 hosting | MIDI-first focus is defensible, but OSC broadens installations |
| Samples | Built-ins plus one user sample, with file limits | Good demo value, modest ownership versus leaders |
| Cross-platform | Native iOS plus a web build | Genuine differentiator if setup parity and sharing become real |

## 3. Market map

ExpressionPad competes across four overlapping layers.

### Layer A — direct expressive controllers

Users choose these when they already have a synth, DAW, hardware module, or host and want a better playing surface.

**GeoShred Control, KB-1, Velocity Keyboard, KeyPad, Midi Poly Grid, WoodTroller, Ribbons, TC-Data.**

The category expectation is no longer merely “sends MIDI.” It includes clear per-note gesture semantics, bend-range guidance, multiple connection methods, host recall, panic/recovery, and often AUv3.

### Layer B — alternative grids and scale-safe instruments

Users choose these to escape piano geometry, play unfamiliar scales confidently, or explore microtonality.

**Musix Pro, Hexatone, iotaTONE, Rubberband, ThumbJam, Isotope, mTonal, ChordPolyPad, Tonality, Entonal Studio.**

This layer has split into two camps:

- **geometry-first, expression-light:** Musix Pro, Hexatone, iotaTONE, Isotope;
- **expression-first, layout-constrained:** Velocity Keyboard, Ribbons, Rubberband, KB-1.

ExpressionPad can credibly own the intersection.

### Layer C — touch-native instruments

These apps compete for the deeper emotional job: “give me an instrument that only makes sense on glass.”

**GeoShred Pro, Animoog Z, MorphWiz Studio, TC-11, ThumbJam, Samplr, Borderlands Granular, SpaceCraft, Fluss, Mononoke, Shoom, iFretless, Steel Guitar PRO, DrumJam, Bebot.**

Their lesson is that a memorable constraint often beats broad configurability. GeoShred’s guitar-derived pitch behavior, Samplr’s direct sample manipulation, Mononoke’s eight interacting drone pads, and TC-11’s gesture synthesis are easy to describe and demonstrate. ExpressionPad needs equally legible signature behaviors.

### Layer D — builders, hosts, and rig control

These products absorb the job when users are willing to assemble their own surface or centralize an entire performance system.

**TouchOSC, MIDI Designer Pro X, Lemur, Surface Builder, Loopy Pro, AUM, LK, Mozaic, Rozeta, Midiflow, MidiFire, StreamByter.**

TouchOSC wins arbitrary layouts, scripting, bidirectional OSC/MIDI, and heterogeneous systems. Loopy Pro and AUM win whole-rig routing and hosting. LK wins Ableton integration and modular one-time purchases. ExpressionPad should integrate with these ecosystems, not attempt to replace them all.

### Closest rival: GeoShred

GeoShred’s positioning—“not just an app, an instrument”—is the standard ExpressionPad must meet. GeoShred has intelligent pitch rounding, scale and tuning depth, multiple expressive modes, a mature preset ecosystem, broad CoreMIDI connectivity, and an AUv3 MIDI sender. GeoShred Control is free; sound requires a current $24.99 functionality upgrade, with additional modeled instruments sold separately. Its surface editor can add controls and map multiple parameters, although its core playing metaphor remains guitar/string-derived. [GeoShred Control App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/geoshred-control/id1336247116), [GeoShred MIDI documentation](https://www.moforte.com/geoShredAssets7000/help/midi.html), [control-surface documentation](https://www.moforte.com/geoShredAssets7000/help/controlSurface.html).

**ExpressionPad can win** on a simpler first minute, broader geometry, the distinctive phone mirror, haptic fret crossings, and one coherent purchase. It cannot win by claiming deeper current ecosystem maturity.

### Broadest new rival: KeyPad

KeyPad is the most important 2026 entrant. It combines piano, isomorphic, drum, and XY surfaces with height-derived velocity, mono/polyphonic aftertouch, MPE pitch and CC74, AUv3, OSC, virtual/network/Bluetooth/wired MIDI, and an engine that loads SF2, SFZ, WAV, and M4A. It is currently free and has a small rating base, so it is more feature threat than validated market leader. [KeyPad App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/keypad-midi-controller/id6758680165), [developer page](https://www.discodsp.com/keypad/).

**ExpressionPad can win** by feeling like one authored instrument instead of a controller suite: better continuity between keys and slides, spring vibrato, haptic landmarks, richer geometry behavior, and clearer visual cause-and-effect.

### Host-workflow benchmark: KB-1

KB-1 offers eight controller templates, up to five independent keyboards in standalone sessions, dedicated outputs, MPE, and AUv3 MIDI operation. It is controller-only and template-based rather than an arbitrary geometry system. Its pressure claim is tied largely to legacy 3D Touch hardware; vertical slide remains useful on modern devices. [KB-1 App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kb-1-keyboard-suite/id1437919435), [KB-1 product page](https://numericalaudio.com/kb1/).

**ExpressionPad can win** on internal sound, play-surface identity, phone ergonomics, and the continuous-to-fretted continuum. KB-1 currently wins session integration.

### Glass-dynamics benchmark: Velocity Keyboard

Velocity Keyboard is narrowly focused on deriving useful attack dynamics and expression from a touchscreen. It supports MPE and AUv3, offers piano, string, scale, and drum layouts, and can map touch area, Y position, velocity, or legacy hardware force into different messages. [Velocity Keyboard App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/velocity-keyboard/id1462605052), [developer page](http://www.bluemangoo.com/vecocity_keyboard.php).

**ExpressionPad should copy the clarity, not the exact algorithm:** publish how velocity is derived, provide curve presets, visualize the result, and allow conventional fixed-velocity fallback.

### Geometry benchmark: Musix Pro

Musix Pro remains unusually relevant because it is both old and newly active. It supports hexagonal and rectangular surfaces, established isomorphic arrangements, custom interval layouts, resizable notes, color/label options, internal sounds, MIDI, and OSC. It does not currently advertise MPE, continuous per-note pitch, aftertouch, or AUv3. [Musix Pro App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/musix-pro-midi-controller/id585857087), [developer site](https://shiverware.com/musixpro/).

**ExpressionPad can win** by showing that its geometry changes how notes move and feel, not only where notes are placed.

### Emerging concept benchmark: Rubberband

Rubberband launched into the same conceptual space in July 2026: a continuous “elastic” pitch field with adjustable attraction from free glissando toward scales, microtonal/world tunings, tilt, haptics, internal sound, looping, and Pro-gated MPE. It is iPhone-first and newly unvalidated, but its proposition is much easier to understand than most controller listings. [Rubberband App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rubberband-microtonal/id6781069946).

**ExpressionPad’s response** should be a stronger demonstration of configurability without losing immediacy: “keys when you want certainty, strings when you want motion, frets when you want both.”

## 5. Touch-native instrument benchmarks

These apps matter because controller buyers still judge the first touch, the sound, and the identity of the instrument before they judge the protocol matrix.

| Product | Current proposition | Lesson for ExpressionPad |
|---|---|---|
| [Animoog Z](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/animoog-z-synthesizer/id1586841361) | Free shell, $14.99 full unlock; multidimensional wavetable/timbre movement, AUv3, MPE | Make gesture-to-sound causality obvious and beautiful |
| [MorphWiz Studio](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/morphwiz-studio/id6739972511) | $19.99; animated pitch space, user samples, AUv3, MPE input | A premium relaunch needs signature presets and visual identity |
| [TC-11](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tc-11/id488577050) | $24.99; deep multitouch and motion synthesis, active in 2026 | Gestures can be the synthesis architecture, not decoration |
| [ThumbJam](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/thumbjam/id338977566) | $8.99; scales, multisamples, user instruments, MPE/OSC | Instant confidence and sound ownership create longevity |
| [Samplr](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/samplr/id560756420) | $19.99; touch-native sample performance, iPad only | A single unforgettable interaction can outweigh checklist gaps |
| [Borderlands Granular](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/borderlands-granular/id561369733) | Spatial granular scenes and direct manipulation | Save/share the entire playable scene, not just a patch |
| [SpaceCraft](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spacecraft-granular-synth/id1391256308) | Focused granular play surface, AUv3, MPE input | Give every axis a stable musical meaning |
| [Mononoke](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mononoke/id1492577124) | $8.99; eight expressive drone pads and separate AUv3 MIDI pads | A separate controller plug-in is a useful architecture precedent |
| [Fluss](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fluss-granular-playground/id6443472888) | Physics-driven granular motion with multiple AUv3 roles | Gesture recording/inertia can extend expression after release |

Three cross-market lessons recur.

First, **stable mappings beat feature counts**. GeoShred’s horizontal pitch and vertical articulation, Mononoke’s eight coherent pads, and SpaceCraft’s location-in-a-recording metaphor are learnable because dimensions retain meaning. ExpressionPad’s default X, Y, tilt, frets, and vibrato semantics should remain stable across sound presets.

Second, **scale safety is an expert feature too**. GeoShred, ThumbJam, Animoog Z, Rubberband, Shoom, and Bebot make aggressive motion safer through attraction, rounding, snapping, or curated pitch fields. ExpressionPad already contains the raw continuum—discrete keys, slide, frets, and in-key vibrato—but needs one visual language that explains it.

Third, **user sound ownership raises the comparison bar**. Animoog Z imports/records timbres; MorphWiz analyzes user samples; ThumbJam builds multisampled instruments; Samplr, Borderlands, SpaceCraft, and Fluss make the user’s audio central. ExpressionPad’s one user sample is valuable, but the next credible step is multiple named slots, in-app recording, trim/loop controls, and portable setups.

## 6. Builders, hosts, and ecosystem expectations

### TouchOSC defines the limit of “customizable controller”

TouchOSC costs $19.99 upfront, runs on iPhone and iPad, includes a complete cross-platform editor, exposes a large widget vocabulary, supports nested layouts, multiple MIDI/OSC routes, bidirectional state, local messages, and Lua scripting, and was updated in July 2026. It does not document native turnkey MPE, AUv3, or internal sound. [TouchOSC App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/touchosc/id1569996730), [product page](https://hexler.net/touchosc), [manual](https://hexler.net/touchosc/manual/introduction).

ExpressionPad should not say “fully customizable MIDI controller” unless it intends to inherit TouchOSC-level expectations. The sharper contrast is:

> **TouchOSC lets you build a control system. ExpressionPad gives you an instrument to play.**

### MIDI Designer Pro X and Lemur own the expert-platform tier

MIDI Designer Pro X offers deep professional layout and MIDI logic through a free shell, premium subscription options, and a high-priced lifetime tier; AUv3 has shipped, while previously announced OSC support was not documented as shipped at this research snapshot. Lemur’s rerelease brought the historically influential scripted physics controller back with subscription and lifetime options, but also illustrated user resistance to subscription-only relaunches. [MIDI Designer Pro X](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/midi-designer-pro-x/id492291712), [MDPx overview](https://mididesigner.com/home/mdpx/), [Lemur App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lemur/id6739544164), [Lemur rerelease statement](https://www.midikinetics.com/announcement/lemur-rerelease/).

The lesson is not to add a scripting language. It is that community layouts, open export, migration, and a believable continuity promise can become the actual moat.

### Loopy Pro and AUM are partners before they are competitors

Loopy Pro is a $29.99 perpetual live-performance environment with an optional update year, deep widgets, AUv3 hosting, looping, sequencing, routing, MIDI learn, and MPE recording. Its widgets are not a documented native per-note MPE surface, and its scope creates setup and cognitive weight. [Loopy Pro pricing](https://loopypro.com/pricing/), [manual](https://loopypro.com/manual/).

AUM is a $20.99 mixer, router, and AUv3 host that can pass the constituent MIDI messages used by MPE, but does not market a native MPE surface. [AUM App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/aum-audio-mixer/id1055636344), [manual](https://kymatica.com/aum/help).

ExpressionPad should publish tested, illustrated AUM and Loopy Pro recipes. Those apps solve routing and recording; ExpressionPad can become the thing players put in front of them.

### LK sets a modular workflow precedent

LK combines Ableton control, an AUv3 MIDI clip matrix, a customizable controller, XY pads, chord tools, and an MPE keyboard. It uses modular one-time IAPs and a discounted bundle, with active 2026 maintenance. [LK App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/lk-ableton-midi-controller/id944972221), [LK product page](https://www.imaginando.pt/products/lk-ableton-live-and-midi-controller).

Its strongest lesson is packaging: destination-specific workflows and reusable JSON layouts make “integration” concrete. ExpressionPad should ship destination profiles before attempting a blank canvas.

### Workstations are value anchors, not surface peers

Logic Pro for iPad, GarageBand, Cubasis, KORG Gadget, Drambo, Koala Sampler, and Zenbeats are not direct substitutes for a continuous MPE surface, but they absorb the broader jobs “make music on iOS” and “give me one app that does enough.” Their conventional keyboards, drum pads, chord strips, XY controls, scale locks, and note-repeat tools make on-screen play table stakes. Their sound libraries make “includes a synth and sampler” a weak standalone claim.

GarageBand is the most severe free anchor: it provides Touch Instruments, Smart Instruments, Live Loops, Alchemy, sampling, effects, downloadable content, AUv3 hosting, and explicit MPE-controller input. [GarageBand product page](https://www.apple.com/ios/garageband/), [MPE guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-ipad/use-mpe-controllers-chsc2cc0d4f8/ipados). Logic is the professional breadth ceiling and a capable MPE destination, not a surface ExpressionPad should imitate. [Logic Pro for iPad](https://www.apple.com/logic-pro/), [play-surface guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro-ipad/intro-to-play-surfaces-lpip9ac51271/ipados).

Drambo is the clearest “build your own instrument” adjacent rival: a $24.99 modular groovebox, AUv3 host/plug-in, and MIDI environment with deep mapping and MPE-aware polyphony. It is also an ideal destination for an authored front end. [Drambo App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/drambo/id1469365718), [developer page](https://www.beepstreet.com/ios/drambo). Koala is the immediacy and value benchmark: a $4.99 sampler whose focused record–tap–resample loop expands through low-cost Samurai and Mixer add-ons. [Koala App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/koala-sampler-beat-maker/id1449584007), [manual](https://manual.koalasampler.com/mobile/10-in-app-purchases/).

The strategic boundary is clear: ExpressionPad should not add tracks, a piano roll, a mixer, or a content store to justify itself. It should make GarageBand, Logic, Drambo, AUM, and Loopy Pro feel better to play.

## 7. App Store economics and merchandising

The [27-app purposive storefront audit](../outputs/ios-music-control-surfaces-research-appstore.md) reveals a niche that still prefers ownership over subscriptions:

- 19 of 27 apps are paid upfront.
- The paid-app median is **$17.99**; the all-app median is **$8.99**.
- In the 13-app direct-controller/expressive-surface segment, 10 are paid and the paid median is **$12.49**.
- Eight apps are free, but six are gateways to IAPs or paid functionality. AudioKit Synth One and GarageBand are the unusual genuinely free products in this set.
- 14 of 27 show IAPs; only two visibly use subscription offers.
- 16 of 27 were updated in the prior 180 days and 22 within the prior year.
- All 27 support iPad; 24 support iPhone.

The viable price bands are:

| Product promise | Legible price | Evidence-based interpretation |
|---|---:|---|

ExpressionPad has a natural answer: the web instrument can be the playable demo and sharing surface, while the native app sells reliability, low latency, haptics, CoreMIDI, and deeper setup ownership.

### Merchandising patterns

The sample had a median of six App Store media assets, but only nine of 27 used any preview video and only Samplr had an iPad preview. Fourteen used benefit-led screenshot headlines. This creates a straightforward opportunity: an expressive surface is intrinsically more compelling in motion than in a static UI, yet the category is underusing video.

Language patterns in the sample were equally revealing:

- “MIDI” appeared in 24 of 27 listings.
- “Controller” appeared in 18.
- “Expressive” appeared in 15.
- “Touch” appeared in 16.
- “AUv3” appeared in 13.
- “MPE” appeared in 10.

These words are necessary for discovery but insufficient for differentiation. “Expressive MPE MIDI controller” describes a shelf, not a reason to care.

### Recommended App Store identity

**Name:** `expressionPad: MPE Controller`  
**Subtitle:** `Touch Instrument & MIDI`

The exact name should be validated in App Store Connect, but the structure is right: preserve the distinctive brand, then add the highest-intent category term. Capitalization must be made consistent across the binary, website, support, screenshots, and press kit.

**One-line value proposition:**

> A configurable touch instrument for iPhone and iPad: slide, bend, vibrate, and shape every note independently—play its built-in synth and sampler or control an MPE rig.

**Screenshot sequence:**

1. **Slide between notes. Shape every voice.**  
   Full-screen hex or square performance surface with visible multi-touch trails.
2. **Every finger bends independently.**  
   A simple X=pitch, Y=expression diagram over a real gesture.
3. **Keys, strings, and frets—one surface.**

4. **Built for both thumbs.**  
   iPhone landscape mirror mode.
5. **Feel every fret. Play without staring.**

6. **Play its sounds—or your whole MPE rig.**

- no analytics unless genuinely needed and separately disclosed;
- local processing;
- samples remain on-device unless the user exports them;
- named support email and compatibility page;
- current version history with real release notes.

## 8. User needs, praise, and recurring failure modes

The strongest user response in this category is not “this replaces my keyboard.” It is “this screen became a new instrument.”

### What users consistently value

1. **Immediate musical safety.** Scale locks, pitch attraction, sensible defaults, and an audible first touch reduce fear without limiting expert motion.
2. **Low, stable latency.** Users notice latency, jitter, stuck notes, and unreliable wireless routing quickly. Marketing numbers without test conditions erode trust.
3. **A coherent physical metaphor.** Ribbons, strings, frets, grains, elastic pitch, or an authored grid are easier to learn than arbitrary mappings.
4. **Project recall.** A setup must return with its routing, bend range, layout, sound, and expression semantics intact.
5. **Developer presence.** Clear support, compatibility notes, updates, and honest answers matter in a niche where unique apps have disappeared.
6. **Portability and ownership.** Exportable presets, local backup, sample ownership, and migration reduce the fear that a practiced instrument will vanish.
7. **Accessibility through design.** Large targets, Direct Touch/VoiceOver workflows, haptic landmarks, hardware-keyboard control, and reduced visual demand can make touchscreen instruments useful to disabled musicians.

### Recurring pain points

#### “MPE support” is ambiguous

An app may generate expressive internal audio, accept MPE, emit MPE, record MPE, replay it correctly, or merely use per-note pitch channels for microtonal tuning. Those are different promises. ExpressionPad should publish an exact signal map and a short tested-destination table:

| Gesture | Default output | User-visible setup |
|---|---|---|
| Touch onset | Note + velocity | velocity curve/fixed mode |
| Horizontal movement | Per-note pitch bend | bend range and snap/fret behavior |
| Vertical movement | Channel pressure and/or CC74 | separate toggles and curves |
| Release | Note off | release behavior |
| Tilt | Chosen global/performance CC | calibration, range, smoothing, off |

#### Routing is a cognitive tax

“Choose a destination” is not enough. Users must align app state, virtual ports, channels, MPE zones, bend ranges, background behavior, host tracks, monitoring, and record settings. A compatibility recipe should include both ends of the connection.

#### Presets are onboarding, not only storage

A blank controller is an empty state. A named preset can teach one musical behavior, establish a known-good destination, and give support a reproducible starting point. The setup is the product unit.

#### AUv3 recall is valuable but not automatically reliable

AUv3 reduces app switching and embeds state in a host session, but users still encounter UI sizing, state restoration, MIDI recording, and host-specific behavior. Shipping AUv3 without a tested host matrix will not close the credibility gap.

#### Glass has no tactile map

The screen lacks key edges, string tension, and independent physical pressure. Competitors use scale safety, large regions, visual feedback, haptics, touch-area proxies, tilt, or Apple Pencil. ExpressionPad’s haptic fret crossings and mirror geometry are excellent responses, but should be tested with eyes-up performance rather than treated as decoration.

#### Continuity fear is justified

The original ExpressionPad is gone from the current US storefront. Pen2Bow, Aftertouch, AC Sabre, and ROLI NOISE are also delisted. Lemur itself passed through end-of-life and later rerelease. Touchscaper entered maintenance mode because its architecture depended on Inter-App Audio. The lesson is larger than “keep updating”: users are investing practice, presets, mappings, samples, and performance confidence.

### Recommended continuity contract

Publish this at launch:

1. setup export uses a documented, versioned, human-readable format;
2. local backups never require an account;
3. migrations are tested and described in release notes;
4. supported iOS versions, hosts, and devices live on a current compatibility page;
5. any future end-of-life includes advance notice and an export path;
6. if maintenance becomes impossible, the team will evaluate open-sourcing the core or another preservation route.

That promise is more strategically valuable here than a large launch sound pack.

## 9. Audience and jobs to be done

### Primary: the expressive iOS player

**Job:** “Give me a playable surface that turns finger motion into phrasing, without making me design a controller.”

This user already understands MPE or has encountered GeoShred, Seaboard, LinnStrument, or expressive AUv3 synths. They value per-note control, predictable pitch, low latency, and host recall.

**Winning proof:** one finger bends while a held chord stays stable; a recorded host clip replays the same performance correctly.

### Primary: the alternative-layout musician

**Job:** “Let me arrange pitch for my hands and harmonic system, rather than inherit a piano.”

This includes guitarists, bassists, grid players, microtonal musicians, and people who think spatially. They compare ExpressionPad with Musix Pro, Hexatone, iotaTONE, GeoShred, Midi Poly Grid, and Entonal.

**Winning proof:** switch the same phrase among fourths, fifths, guitar tuning, hex, and stacked piano; save the result as one setup.

### Primary: the phone-first musical sketcher and performer

**Job:** “Turn the device I always have into a credible two-handed instrument.”

Many sophisticated competitors are iPad-first, controller-only, or awkward on a phone. Mirror mode is unusually specific to this job.

**Winning proof:** two-thumb bass/lead or inward scale performance in phone landscape, with controls collapsed and haptic pitch landmarks.

### Secondary: the iOS producer

**Job:** “Give me a better keyboard inside AUM, Loopy Pro, Logic, or another host, and remember it with the project.”

This audience is reachable only weakly until AUv3 and project recall exist.

**Winning proof:** insert ExpressionPad, load a named destination profile, record MPE, close the project, reopen it, and resume without repair.

### Secondary: the sound explorer

**Job:** “Let me discover expressive sounds before I understand a routing matrix.”

This user compares ExpressionPad with Animoog Z, TC-11, MorphWiz, ThumbJam, and experimental touch synths.

**Winning proof:** seven to twelve signature instruments in which slide, vibrato, vertical expression, tilt, and haptics produce immediately legible results.

### Secondary: educators and accessible-music practitioners

**Job:** “Give learners or disabled musicians large, configurable, scale-safe regions with dependable landmarks.”

This is not won with a generic accessibility claim. It requires VoiceOver semantics, Direct Touch testing, high contrast, reduced motion, hardware-keyboard completeness, optional audio announcements, and documentation co-created with actual users.

## 10. Positioning

### Recommended category

**Continuous touch instrument and MPE controller**

This is more specific than “music app,” more experiential than “MIDI controller,” and less restrictive than “keyboard.”

### Positioning statement

For musicians who want more expression from an iPhone or iPad than a glass piano can provide, ExpressionPad is a configurable continuous instrument that lets every finger slide, bend, vibrate, and shape its own note. Unlike generic controller builders or fixed expressive keyboards, it unifies square, hex, and piano geometries, tactile fret landmarks, phone-first two-thumb play, immediate sound, and MPE control in one playable system.

### Message hierarchy

1. **Every finger becomes an instrument.**
2. **Slide between notes. Shape every voice.**
3. **Keys, strings, and frets on the screen you already own.**
4. **Play its synth and sampler—or control an MPE rig.**
5. **Square, hex, piano, or your own tuning.**
6. **Built for ten fingers on iPad and both thumbs on iPhone.**

### Claims to avoid

- “Fully customizable controller.” This invites comparison with TouchOSC/Lemur-level arbitrary layout, scripting, feedback, and routing.
- “Pressure-sensitive.” Modern iPhones and iPads do not provide independent finger-force sensing; ExpressionPad derives expression from vertical motion.
- “Low latency” without a device, OS, buffer, audio route, and measurement method.
- “MPE compatible” without specifying output direction, messages, zones, bend range, and tested destinations.
- “Background audio” until documentation and lifecycle code agree.
- “Professional” as a substitute for proof.
- “The first” or “the only” unless the claim is narrowed and re-verified immediately before release.

### Competitive response by rival

| Rival | Do not attack | Demonstrate instead |
|---|---|---|
| GeoShred | Its guitar heritage or depth | Faster first minute, broader geometry, phone mirror, simpler purchase |
| KeyPad | Its youth or small ratings | More coherent instrument behavior and tactile identity |
| KB-1 | Lack of sound alone | Full setup recall, immediate sound, continuous-to-fretted play |
| Velocity | “Fake” velocity | Transparent curves, clear visuals, musical defaults |

## 11. Strategic whitespace

### 1. The geometry–expression intersection

The active market has many grid layouts and many expressive surfaces, but few combine deeply configurable pitch geometry with independently continuous per-note movement. This is the strongest core whitespace.

**Product expression:** a setup model where geometry, scale/tuning, slide/fret behavior, expression curves, sound, FX, and destination profile are saved together.

### 2. Phone-first two-hand ergonomics

Many “universal” apps merely fit an iPhone. ExpressionPad’s inward mirror is an actual phone performance technique. Treat it as a named mode, create dedicated presets, and commission players to develop recognizable two-thumb vocabulary.

### 3. Eyes-up glass performance

Haptic fret crossings can address the missing tactile map more musically than generic vibration. Add haptic strength/character presets, audio calibration, and a training mode that gradually removes labels or visual cues. Test whether users can execute slides and return to pitch without looking.

### 4. Progressive pitch assistance

Competitors typically choose discrete keys, scale snap, intelligent rounding, or a continuous field. ExpressionPad can expose one understandable continuum:

**keys → glide → frets → attraction → free slide**

The repository already contains much of this logic. A single performance control and visualization would be more legible than separate technical toggles.

### 5. Web-to-native continuity

A playable web build is rare in this niche. It can become:

- no-install trial;
- setup preview;
- share link;
- documentation sandbox;
- community gallery;
- fallback/preservation layer.

This advantage remains latent until setup files are portable and the web/native state model is intentionally versioned.

### 6. Honest MPE guidance

The category often uses “MPE” loosely. ExpressionPad can win trust with a visual signal inspector, bend-range test, destination presets, and a public compatibility table.

### 7. Accessibility as instrument design

Haptics, scale safety, large regions, collapsible controls, hardware keyboard layouts, and configurable contrast are unusually useful foundations. A tested accessible performance mode could be both socially meaningful and commercially distinctive in an under-documented category.

## 13. Product roadmap

### P0 — correctness and trust before launch

These are release blockers, not growth features.

1. **Resolve background behavior.** Decide whether audio and MIDI should continue, whether the app should remain silent but routable, or whether background is explicitly unsupported. Align entitlements, AVAudioSession category/options, scene lifecycle, panic behavior, and docs.
2. **Run an MPE conformance matrix.** Test at minimum GeoShred, Animoog Z, Moog Mariana/Model 15 if available, Roli-compatible synths, AUM, Loopy Pro, Logic Pro for iPad, and representative hardware. Cover pitch range, channel zone, CC74, pressure, voice stealing, sustain, panic, destination changes, cable disconnect, Bluetooth reconnect, and app interruption.
3. **Add performance safety.** Persistent connection indicator, output name, active voice count, panic, stuck-note cleanup, route-change recovery, and optional “last MIDI message” diagnostics.
4. **Use precise terminology.** Rename player-facing “pressure” to “vertical expression” or “aftertouch.” Keep protocol labels precise in the MIDI inspector.
5. **Unify identity.** Decide `ExpressionPad` versus `expressionPad`; use one form everywhere.
6. **Publish support, privacy, and compatibility pages.**
7. **Measure latency reproducibly.** Publish method and a range, not a universal number.

### P1 — launch credibility

1. **Named complete setups**
   - save, save as, duplicate, rename, delete;
   - factory versus user separation;
   - setup includes surface, tuning, expression, sound/sample, FX, MIDI bend range, and destination profile;
   - versioned JSON export/import;
   - Files/iCloud Drive sharing;
   - recovery from incompatible or partial files.
2. **AUv3**
   - ideally an AUv3 instrument and an AUv3 MIDI controller;
   - if one must ship first, prioritize AUv3 MIDI for host recall and external-instrument value, while preserving immediate audition sound in standalone;
   - test state restoration and UI at compact host sizes.
3. **First-run playable tutorial**
   - sound on first touch;
   - teach tap, horizontal bend, vertical expression, frets, and full-screen mode;
   - offer “play now” and “connect a synth” paths;
   - never block first sound on MIDI permission or account creation.
4. **Destination profiles**
   - AUM;
   - Loopy Pro;
   - Logic Pro for iPad;
   - GeoShred/GeoShred Studio;
   - Animoog Z;
   - common hardware MPE zone.
5. **Accessible performance mode**
   - VoiceOver labels and Direct Touch flow;
   - high contrast and color-independent pitch cues;
   - reduced ripples/motion;
   - large controls;
   - complete external-keyboard navigation;
   - haptic/audio landmarks.

### P2 — strengthen the moat

1. **Microtonality without a theory-lab detour**
   - Scala `.scl` import;
   - EDO generator;
   - arbitrary cents/ratios;
   - explicit equave and A4/reference pitch;
   - share these inside setups.
2. **Full MPE input**
   - make the internal synth/sampler a credible destination;
   - show incoming dimensions and zone;
   - record/replay test cases.
3. **Deeper sample ownership**
   - multiple named user slots;
   - in-app recording;
   - trim, root note, loop start/end/crossfade;
   - portable sample references or bundled export with clear size rules.
4. **Gesture capture**
   - record a short motion or modulation lane;
   - loop, overdub, clear, and scale depth;
   - keep it musical and deterministic rather than becoming a general automation editor.
5. **Community setup gallery**
   - curated, signed factory/community packs;
   - web preview;
   - QR/share link;
   - compatibility and version metadata.
6. **Optional OSC**
   - after MIDI/AUv3 quality is excellent;
   - prioritize Max/Pd, SuperCollider, TouchDesigner, Resolume, theater, and installations;
   - avoid promising TouchOSC-like arbitrary control construction.

### P3 — expansion, not launch blockers

- MIDI 2.0/UMP after real destination demand and a clear migration benefit;
- Apple Pencil pressure/tilt as an optional specialized mode;
- Ableton Link if tempo-synced gesture capture, arpeggiation, or looping ships;
- cross-device sync/control;
- splits with independently routed zones;
- a richer sampler or clip surface only if research shows users want ExpressionPad to become a workstation.

## 14. Packaging and pricing

### Recommended model

**Preferred:** free playable download with a one-time **$14.99 Pro unlock**.

The free tier should be genuinely musical:

- several signature factory setups;
- internal sound;
- limited but useful layouts;
- one MIDI destination;
- no time pressure or subscription nag during play.

Pro should unlock durable professional value:

- all layout/tuning controls;
- full MIDI/MPE routing and destination profiles;
- named setup library and import/export;
- AUv3;
- user samples and advanced sound controls;
- future core features within the major version.

This model counters free entry from GeoShred Control, KeyPad, Animoog Z, and Rubberband while letting the interaction sell itself.

### Simpler alternative

If maintaining a tiered product is undesirable, price the complete AUv3-ready app at **$14.99 paid upfront** and use the web version as the unrestricted demo. The website should deep-link from a shared preset directly to the App Store.

### Avoid

- subscription for core play, MIDI, setups, or AUv3;
- many small unlocks for essential expression dimensions;
- an “intro price” without a published end date;
- pricing at $19.99–$24.99 before the internal sound library, sample workflow, host integration, and setup ownership feel complete.

Optional sound or artist setup packs are category-consistent later, provided the base product remains whole and exports continue to work without cloud dependence.

## 17. Metrics

Traditional download metrics are insufficient for an instrument. Track the funnel around successful play and durable ownership, ideally on-device or with privacy-preserving opt-in telemetry.

### Activation

- time to first audible note;
- percentage reaching full-screen play;
- percentage trying horizontal bend and vertical expression;
- percentage loading a second setup;
- percentage completing a MIDI connection;
- percentage recording a correct per-note expressive phrase in a host.

### Retention and depth

- setups created/exported;
- median number of performance sessions per active device;
- share-link/setup imports;
- internal sound versus external MIDI use;
- mirror-mode usage on phones;
- crash-free and stuck-note-free sessions;
- support requests per connection type/destination.

### Commercial

- web-demo-to-App-Store click-through;
- free-to-Pro conversion, if used;
- conversion after a successful MIDI connection versus before;
- review volume and rating recency;
- tutorial completion and destination-profile downloads.

Avoid collecting raw MIDI performances, imported sample content, or identifiable setup names by default.

## 18. Open questions and required hands-on tests

Documentary research cannot settle these:

1. How does ExpressionPad’s measured touch-to-sound and touch-to-MIDI latency compare with GeoShred, KB-1, Velocity, KeyPad, and ThumbJam on the same device and route?
2. Does Y-drag expression remain controllable during diagonal slides, or do pitch and timbre feel coupled?
3. How many simultaneous independently expressive voices remain reliable before voice stealing becomes audible or MIDI channels exhaust?
4. Does the haptic tick land close enough to the audible pitch crossing to become a landmark rather than noise?
5. Can users understand mirror mode without explanation, and what repertoire actually benefits?
6. Which hosts record and replay pitch, pressure, and CC74 correctly, and which require non-obvious track settings?
7. Does a separate AUv3 MIDI controller provide better UX than a combined instrument/MIDI extension?
8. What setup state can be safely shared between web and native when sample files or unavailable destinations are involved?
9. Can VoiceOver and Direct Touch coexist with the custom pad view without compromising performance?
10. Do musicians want Scala/EDO primarily for imported tunings, custom layout design, or compatibility with existing microtonal synths?
11. Is the internal synth compelling enough to justify instrument pricing, or is it best treated as an audition and onboarding engine?
12. Which current iPhone sizes make mirrored two-thumb play comfortable, and how should safe areas/camera islands affect layout?

## 19. Bottom line

ExpressionPad should not try to out-build TouchOSC, out-theorize Entonal, out-model GeoShred, or out-host Loopy Pro. Its opportunity is more particular and more interesting:

> **Make a flat screen feel like a configurable, continuous instrument—then make that instrument dependable everywhere MIDI musicians already work.**

The product already has the beginnings of a distinctive technique: lattice choice, continuous-to-fretted motion, spring vibrato, haptic landmarks, and phone mirror play. The commercial task is to make those behaviors immediately audible, recallable, recordable, and trustworthy.

AUv3, complete setup ownership, exact MPE guidance, and pre-launch lifecycle correctness are the price of entry. The moat is the playing technique.

### Historical or unavailable landmarks

- **Original ExpressionPad:** no current US Apple record; relevant to the relaunch and continuity story.
- **Pen2Bow:** Apple Pencil bowing controller, delisted in 2026.
- **Aftertouch • 3D MIDI Controller:** highly direct X/Y/Z/MPE comparator, now unavailable and dependent on legacy 3D Touch.
- **AC Sabre:** theatrical motion/MPE phone controller, delisted.
- **ROLI NOISE:** consumerized five-dimensional expression vocabulary, now unavailable in the US.
- **Seaboard 5D:** legacy app; true “Press” depended on now-obsolete 3D Touch devices.
- **Classic Lemur:** historically central; the brand has returned under a new app and commercial model, so it should not be described simply as dead.

## Appendix B — evidence cautions

- Prices, versions, and availability are a US snapshot, not permanent attributes.
- Apple search snippets and third-party mirrors were often stale; live Apple pages/API overruled them.
- “No documented support” is not equivalent to technically impossible.
- Ratings for 2026 entrants are extremely thin; product direction matters more than traction at this stage.
- App Store compatibility does not prove good layout on every device.
- Protocol checklists do not prove reliable real-time performance.
- Historical community anecdotes are used as design signals, not quantified prevalence.
